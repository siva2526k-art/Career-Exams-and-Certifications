# Central Government Marking Systems & Negative Marking Protocols

> **Standardized assessment mechanisms, negative marking penalties, normalization formulas, and minimum qualifying criteria.**

## 1. Negative Marking Paradigms
- **One-Third (1/3rd = 0.33) Deduction:** Standard in UPSC (Prelims), SSC (Tier-1 & Tier-2), RRB, and GATE 1-mark questions. For every incorrect answer, one-third of the positive mark assigned to that question is deducted.
- **One-Fourth (0.25) Deduction:** Standard in Banking (IBPS, SBI, RBI), where 0.25 marks are deducted per wrong answer for 1-mark questions.
- **Two-Thirds (2/3rd = 0.66) Deduction:** In GATE 2-mark multiple-choice questions, an incorrect answer incurs a penalty of 0.66 marks.
- **Zero Negative Marking:**
  - Multiple Select Questions (MSQ) in GATE (All correct options must be selected; no partial credit; no negative marking).
  - Numerical Answer Type (NAT) in GATE.
  - Descriptive mains examination papers.

## 2. Multi-Shift Equi-Percentile Normalization
When an examination is conducted across multiple shifts or days with varying difficulty levels, normalized scores are computed using the standard DoPT / SSC mathematical formula:

$$\widehat{M}_{ij} = \frac{\bar{M}_t^g - M_q^g}{\bar{M}_{ti} - M_{iq}} (M_{ij} - M_{iq}) + M_q^{gm}$$

Where:
- $\widehat{M}_{ij}$ is the normalized mark of the $j^{\text{th}}$ candidate in the $i^{\text{th}}$ shift.
- $\bar{M}_t^g$ is the average marks of top 0.1% candidates across all shifts.
- $M_q^g$ is the sum of mean and standard deviation of marks of all candidates across all shifts.
- $\bar{M}_{ti}$ is the average marks of top 0.1% candidates in the $i^{\text{th}}$ shift.
- $M_{iq}$ is the sum of mean and standard deviation of the $i^{\text{th}}$ shift.
- $M_{ij}$ is the actual raw mark obtained by the candidate.
